How to care for your technical gear

Repairing instead of replacing starts with how you wash. A poorly cared-for ripstop fabric or DWR water-repellent finish loses its function long before it actually wears out. This guide is short on purpose: these are the habits that extend your gear's lifespan the most.

Before washing

  • Close velcro and zippers so they don't rub or snag other garments inside the drum.
  • Empty the pockets — chalk left inside the wash leaves residue on the whole load.
  • Turn the garment inside out: it protects the treated (DWR) outer layer from direct friction with the drum.

Washing

  • Cold or lukewarm water (30°C max), gentle cycle.
  • No fabric softener or bleach: softener clogs the fabric's pores and kills the DWR; bleach degrades technical fibers.
  • Mild detergent, in a small amount — too much detergent also reduces water repellency.
  • Never dry-clean unless the garment's label explicitly says to.

Drying

  • Air dry, away from direct heat sources (radiator, full sun) and constant friction.
  • If the garment allows it, a short low-heat dryer cycle reactivates the DWR better than air drying — check the label first.

When to re-waterproof

If water no longer beads up and starts to "wet" the fabric instead of rolling off, the DWR has worn off — it doesn't mean the garment is broken. A re-waterproofing spray or wash-in treatment (available at any outdoor store) usually restores the function without replacing the garment.

Between uses

  • Store your gear dry and loose, not compressed for months — technical fabric recovers worse from prolonged compression than cotton does.
  • Check seams and reinforcements after an intense season: catching a loose seam early is what the 5-year warranty covers.
